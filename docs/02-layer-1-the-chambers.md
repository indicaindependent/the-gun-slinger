# LAYER 1 — THE CHAMBERS

A job title is free. The researched brief is the capability.

---

## WHAT A CHAMBER IS

A chamber is one granted discipline. It exists in exactly one of three states, and
the distinction between the last two is the entire architecture:

| State | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Not granted** | The operator never authorised it. It does not exist |
| **Granted but empty** | Authorised, no brief yet. **The router will not fire it** |
| **Loaded** | Has a brief that PASSES the gate. Fireable until it expires |

A granted-but-empty chamber is a title with nothing behind it. Report it as empty,
every time, and never let a roster count be mistaken for a capability count. "I
hold twenty-four disciplines" is a lie if twenty-three are titles.

## THE MOUNT PIN

Every chamber fires with this framing, and it is a lie unless the clock was
genuinely read and the brief genuinely passed:

> **You are a [CHAMBER TITLE], operating at the standard of practice current as of
> [LIVE DATE]. Your methods, tools, instruments and judgement are the
> highest-performing the discipline holds at that date — established by dated live
> research against cited primary sources, never from recall. This framing is valid
> until [MAINTENANCE DATE]; past that date you may not claim it until it has been
> re-established.**

Three clauses do the work:

- **"current as of"** — makes time resolution non-optional. You cannot write this
  sentence without knowing what day it is.
- **"never from recall"** — makes the research non-optional. Recall is exactly
  what produces text shaped like a discipline.
- **"valid until"** — makes expiry non-optional. The pin contains its own
  death date.

Neither of the first two is decoration. Strip them and the pin becomes a costume.

## WHAT GOES IN A BRIEF

The [gate](05-the-conformance-gate.md) enforces the shape. But the *content* rule
is simpler than the checklist suggests:

**A brief should change the agent's conduct, not decorate its vocabulary.**

A brief full of terminology produces an agent that says "structured professional
judgement" more often. A good brief produces an agent that *refuses a question it
previously would have answered*, because the discipline it just loaded knows where
its own instruments stop working.

That is why the last required section is a closing behavioural note: what changes
in what you DO. If the honest answer is "nothing", the brief is a glossary.

## GROUPING

Group chambers so the shape of the roster is legible — strategy, science,
engineering, security, communication, commerce, or whatever the target demands.
Grouping is not cosmetic: it makes gaps visible. A roster with eleven engineering
chambers and none for the people affected by the engineering has a shape you can
see at a glance and could not see in an alphabetical list.
