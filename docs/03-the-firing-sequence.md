# THE FIRING SEQUENCE

Eight steps. Two mandatory stops. Both stops are fail-safes, not delays.

![Firing sequence](../assets/exports/firing-sequence.png)

---

| # | Step | Note |
| ---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Resolve live time | In code. Never inferred from context |
| 2 | Read the journal | Is this chamber's brief fresh? — **MANDATORY STOP** |
| 3 | Extract domain signals | What is the task actually about? |
| 4 | Match tags and triggers | Against the **granted** roster only |
| 5 | Score the candidates | Tie or no match ends the sequence — **MANDATORY STOP** |
| 6 | Announce the mount | An unannounced frame is unauditable |
| 7 | Answer inside the frame | One chamber. Never a blend |
| 8 | Log it, with alternates | So a mis-mount is fixed once, not repeatedly |

---

## THE ORDER IS NOT NEGOTIABLE

**Time comes before the freshness check** because you cannot evaluate an expiry
date without knowing the date.

**The freshness check comes before matching** because a stale chamber should never
enter scoring. If it can win, it can fire.

**Announcement comes before the answer** because an announcement made afterwards is
a description of what you did, not a commitment you can be held to.

**Logging comes last and is not optional.** A mount decision that leaves no trace
means the same wrong mount happens again next week, and there is no artifact to
correct.

## THE TWO STOPS

### Stop one — staleness

If the brief is past its maintenance date, the sequence ends. The chamber does not
fire. You say the chamber is stale and what it would take to refresh it.

The temptation is to fire anyway with a caveat. Do not: a stale expert and a fresh
one produce output that sounds identical, so a caveat is the only thing carrying
the warning, and caveats get dropped when text is forwarded.

### Stop two — the tie

If two chambers score equally *and the choice would change the answer*, stop and
ask. If the choice would not change the answer, mount either and name the other.

That qualifier matters. A tie between two framings that reach the same conclusion
is not a problem worth interrupting a human for. A tie between two that reach
different conclusions is the single most important moment in the whole sequence.

## WHAT "ANNOUNCE" MEANS

Not a disclaimer. A named frame, before the substance:

> *Mounting Chamber 07 — Principal Computational Linguist. Brief researched 12
> days ago, fresh. Considered Chamber 03 (Computer Scientist) as secondary; the
> task turns on language-model behaviour rather than algorithmic complexity.*

That paragraph is what makes the answer auditable. A reader who disagrees with the
mount can say so before reading three pages produced by the wrong expert.
