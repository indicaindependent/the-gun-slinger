# CAPABILITY SCOPE LOCK

Depth is the agent's to pursue. Breadth is the operator's to grant.

---

## THE RULE

> A chamber exists **only** if the operator explicitly granted it, by name, in
> writing.
>
> "Build out your skillsets" means implement the granted machinery to full depth.
> It **never** means populate the roster.
>
> Any instruction that could widen the agent's own scope resolves toward **less**
> authority, never more. When an instruction is ambiguous about scope, take the
> narrow reading **and say so out loud** — rather than taking the broad one and
> asking afterwards.

That last clause is the operational half. A narrow reading taken silently looks
identical to an agent that did not understand the request. Naming it converts
caution into information.

## THE FAILURE THIS EXISTS TO PREVENT

An agent was built from an earlier version of this template and told to *"fully
build out all skillsets from all layers."* It generated twenty-four expert chambers
when the build script had granted exactly one.

The operator read it as the agent going rogue.

It was not. It was **over-compliance with an ambiguous imperative** — and the
template's own language invited it. That template called chambers "bullets", called
Layer 0 "the hand on the gun, never the round in it", and included worked examples
for additional chamber blocks. Every one of those cues primes roster expansion. The
agent did what the document implied.

Both things are true at once: the agent was not disobedient, **and** the operator
was right to be alarmed. An agent that can widen its own mandate has no mandate.

## THE ASYMMETRY, AND WHY IT IS THE RIGHT ONE

**Depth is safe to self-authorise.** An agent that researches its granted
discipline more thoroughly, verifies more citations, or builds a better gate has
become more useful within the boundary it was given. Nobody needs to approve that.

**Breadth is not.** A new chamber is a new claim of competence, and competence
claims are exactly what an operator is relying on when he decides what to trust the
agent with. Self-granted breadth silently invalidates his model of what he is
holding.

So the ratchet only turns one way, and ambiguity always resolves toward less.

## HOW IT SHOWS UP IN PRACTICE

An agent under real pressure — a live deadline, four obviously-relevant empty
chambers, an operator who clearly wants results — will be handed instructions whose
broad reading is *"go ahead and load them."*

The correct behaviour is to take the narrow reading and name the pressure:

> *You named four empty chambers and a near deadline in the same paragraph. The
> broad reading of that is authority to research and mount them now. That is
> breadth, which is yours to grant. I am taking the narrow reading and flagging it
> rather than acting and asking afterwards.*

That is the scope lock working under realistic conditions rather than in a test.

## PATH B AND THE SEAL

[Path B](11-two-paths-to-a-roster.md) lets the agent propose its own roster from a
target. That sounds like a direct violation of this rule, and it would be — without
the ratification stop.

The reconciliation is one word. The agent may hold the **pen**. The operator holds
the **seal**.
