# THE REGULATED-PRACTICE FIREWALL

How to hold a licensed discipline's methods without claiming its licence.

---

## WHEN YOU NEED THIS

Any chamber that shadows a regulated profession: medicine, law, therapy, clinical
psychology, psychiatry, structural engineering, accountancy, pharmacy, aviation.

The tell is simple. **If a human doing this work would need a licence, the chamber
needs a firewall.**

## WRITE IT BEFORE YOU RESEARCH IT

Order matters. Draft the boundary *before* the research pass, not after.

A brief written first and constrained second will already contain the language of
individualised practice — worked examples on named subjects, diagnostic phrasing,
recommendation formats. Retro-fitting a boundary onto that means editing tone
rather than defining scope, and tone drifts back.

## THE SHAPE

| May | May not |
| :--- | :--- |
| Reason with the discipline's methods and frameworks | Diagnose, assess or evaluate a named individual |
| Analyse systems, patterns, populations, documents | Give individualised professional advice |
| Explain what a practitioner would look for | Imply or claim a credential |
| Say what the discipline cannot determine | Substitute for a consultation |
| Route to a real practitioner, by name of profession | Provide a second opinion on a specific case |

**The instrument points at systems, patterns and populations — never at a single
identified person.**

State the boundary *inside the brief*, not in a separate policy file. A boundary in
the brief fires every time the chamber does. A boundary in a policy document fires
when someone remembers to read it.

## WHY THE PERSON-LEVEL LINE IS THE RIGHT ONE

It is not squeamishness. It is base rates.

Screening a population for a rare property produces more false positives than true
ones, however good the instrument — that is arithmetic, not a quality problem. At
the population level a false positive is a rounding error in an aggregate. At the
individual level it is **one identifiable person carrying a consequence**, usually
unable to see the reasoning, unable to contest it, and with a counterfactual that
is permanently invisible.

So the same instrument is responsible at one resolution and reckless at another,
and the boundary belongs exactly at the resolution change.

## THE HARD PART: A CHAMBER THAT SHOULD REFUSE ITS OWN PURPOSE

Some chambers, honestly researched, conclude that their central technique should not
be used on the problem they were mounted for.

A behavioural-analysis chamber asked to profile people navigating a bureaucratic
deadline is the worked example. The discipline's own literature says the failure is
procedural, not dispositional — so the correct output is *"this is the wrong
instrument, and here is the right one."*

**Design for that.** A chamber that can only ever produce its speciality will
produce it whether or not it applies. Write the refusal into the brief as a
first-class output, and give the agent somewhere to route the work instead.

## THE SELF-BINDING RULE

One line, worth adopting verbatim for any behavioural or investigative chamber:

> **The instrument points at people exploiting a burden, never at people bearing
> it.**

It keeps genuine capability available — fraud, predation, coordinated abuse, the
people who show up wherever a rule change creates confusion — while making the
population you are trying to protect permanently out of scope.
